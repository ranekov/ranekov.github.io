Hello, I’m Ranekov.

There was a period in the past when I was in an extremely unstable state of mind for about a year.

I thought about suicide almost every day, repeatedly hurt myself, and felt intense distrust and hostility toward the people around me.

Even looking back now, I can’t call my mental state at the time normal.

This time, I’d like to write about a plan I came up with during that period.

The plan was nicknamed the “murder flea market.”

As the name suggests, it was an attempt to create an online platform that would broker murders.

It’s pretty disturbing, but to me back then, it was no joke.

Just so it’s clear, this article looks back on my past experiences and the way I thought at the time. I’m not trying to condone crime or encourage anyone to commit it. I also won’t discuss any specific ways the plan could have been carried out.

---

## When my mental health began to unravel

At the time, I was dealing with all kinds of stress, including entrance exams and pressure from my parents.

As it all piled up, one day I suddenly started hyperventilating on my way to school.

I froze in the middle of the road and couldn’t move.

I was terrified of the people and things around me—of everything I saw.

Even though I knew rationally that there was no danger, I couldn’t shake the feeling that everything around me was coming to kill me.

I didn’t even know what I was afraid of.
I didn’t know what I was supposed to run from.

I was just utterly terrified of being there.

Somehow, I managed to get home and took refuge under my futon.

Being under the covers made me feel a little safer. I’d barely had a chance to think I was finally somewhere safe when my mother started talking to me about my future.

“Are you really going to be okay in the future if you keep this up?”

I don’t remember her exact words, but they felt incredibly heavy to me at the time.

The entrance exams, my future, what I was going to do from then on.

After listening to her talk for an hour or two, I reached my mental breaking point.

I didn’t want to think about anything anymore.
I didn’t want to suffer any longer.
I just wanted to feel better, right then and there.

I was overwhelmed by those feelings.

That was when I remembered my mother had once mentioned cutting myself.

“If this can make me feel even a little better…”

Thinking that, I started hurting myself.

And for a while, I did feel better.

Of course, none of my problems had been solved.

It was just as if, for a moment, the pain that had filled my mind had been replaced by something else.

To me at the time, it came to feel like one of the few ways I could escape.

After that, I started hurting myself repeatedly.

I regret that I’m left with scars.

That’s just how cornered I was back then.

---

## Thoughts of killing my mother and wanting to die

At the time, the entrance exams felt like they would determine my entire life.

With the pressure from my parents, I became firmly convinced that

“If I fail the entrance exams, there’s nothing to hope for in the future.”

And I arrived at an extreme conclusion.

“If I fail the entrance exams, I’ll kill my mother and then kill myself.”

Looking back now, it was an incredibly dangerous and selfish thought.

But I genuinely believed it at the time.

The results of the exams weren’t even out yet.

In other words, faced with a future in which nothing had been decided, I was already laying out murder and suicide as options.

If I failed the exams, it would all be over.
So I might as well end everything then.

That was how extreme my thinking had become.

“I’m going to die anyway.”
“I have nothing left to lose.”

I think those feelings of giving up had warped my judgment—not only about my own life, but about other people’s lives, too.

As time went on, I grew more distrustful of people in my daily life, and began to feel disgust toward humanity itself.

Eventually, I came to think that the world would be better off if the entire human race went extinct.

And gradually, my thoughts took an even more extreme turn.

“How could I kill as many people as possible?”

Looking back now, it’s horrifying that my thoughts went that far.

But at the time, I couldn’t stop them.

## Why I came up with the “murder flea market”

What I ultimately came up with wasn’t committing a crime on my own, but creating a platform to connect people who wanted murders carried out.

In other words, instead of carrying out the killings myself, I was trying to create a system that would involve other people’s intentions and actions.

At the time, I thought that an individual committing a crime and a system that brokered it were completely different things.

Rather than ending it with my own actions, I wanted to create a system that other people could take part in, too.

Looking back now, it was a profoundly warped idea.

I was also intensely interested in anonymity on the internet.

The internet makes it possible for people to connect across physical distances and national borders.

I think I was interested in that aspect of it, too.

I won’t go into what technology I was planning to use or exactly how I thought I could make it happen.

That’s because what I want to talk about in this article isn’t the technical concept, but how I came to think up such a plan in the first place.

That was how the project nicknamed the “murder flea market” came about.

---

## The “murder flea market” concept

The idea was an online platform that would connect people seeking to have someone killed with people willing to carry it out.

The concept was like taking an ordinary flea market or matchmaking service and repurposing it for crime.

The main features I had in mind at the time were:

- **Matching:** A system for connecting clients with people who would carry out the murders.
- **Funds management:** A system for managing money related to the transactions.
- **Anonymity:** A concept centered on keeping users’ identities hidden.
- **Rating system:** A system for building trust through user ratings.
- **Online operation:** The idea of providing the service over the internet.

Looking back now, it was as if I were trying to take the mechanics of an ordinary flea market and apply them directly to a marketplace for human lives.

At the time, all I could think about was combining features like these.

But the very idea of treating human life as something to be traded is fundamentally wrong.

Unlike ordinary commercial transactions, this involves people who would suffer irreparable harm.

I think I was so focused on how the service would work and its technical aspects that I couldn’t properly think about that fact.

---

## Why a flea market?

One thing I find personally interesting is why I wanted to make murder work like a flea market.

At the time, I was interested in ideas like utilitarianism and hedonism.

What especially resonated with me was the idea that

“the ends matter, not the means.”

As a result, I tended to focus more on the final outcome and efficiency than on the process of achieving something.

I think that way of thinking was reflected in the plan.

In a typical flea market, users make transactions with one another and build trust through ratings.

At the time, I thought I might be able to apply a system like that to another area.

Systematize exchanges between individuals and manage them through a platform.

Looking back now, the very idea of treating human life as just another thing to trade is fundamentally broken.

It’s wrong even before you get to the question of whether it could work as a system.

At the time, I thought only about what was technically possible.

But what you can do and what you should do are completely different things.

I think I’d lost even the ability to tell the difference.

---

## The patterns in my thinking at the time

Looking back on this plan, I can see several distinctive tendencies in the way I thought back then.

### 1. An excessive focus on outcomes and efficiency

I prioritized achieving my goals over the process and ethical concerns.

If something would get the job done, the means didn’t matter.

I think that way of thinking had taken hold of me.

Now I understand that if the goal itself is wrong, it doesn’t matter how efficient the means are.

### 2. Distrust of other people

As I became more and more mentally cornered, I stopped being able to trust other people.

I saw the people around me as enemies and felt intense disgust toward society itself.

It seems that my frustrations with individuals had somehow grown into disgust toward all of humanity.

### 3. A collapse in how I valued my own life

At the time, I had almost no resistance to the idea of dying.

The fact that I had little regard for my own life may have been one reason my judgment about other people’s lives was warped, too.

It didn’t matter what happened to me.

I think that feeling led me to even more extreme ways of thinking.

### 4. Seeing everything in extreme either-or terms

Pass the entrance exams or your life is over.

Friend or enemy.

Live or die.

At the time, I saw everything in extreme either-or terms.

In reality, even if I had failed the entrance exams, there would have been plenty of other paths in life.

I could have gone to a different school or found another way to think about my future.

But back then, I could barely see any of those options.

Looking back, I must have had a very narrow perspective.

---

## So what happened to the plan?

The short answer is that the plan was never completed.

I thought about it seriously at the time, but never got as far as launching or operating it as a real service.

Looking at it from where I am now, I’m truly glad it was never completed.

Back then, I was directing my technical interests and abilities in a completely wrong direction.

Building servers, writing programs, or thinking about complex systems isn’t bad in itself.

The problem is what I was trying to use those things for.

Technology can be used in all sorts of ways.

The same abilities can help people, or cause irreparable harm, depending on how they’re used.

When I think about it that way, what happened back then has become an important lesson for me.

---

## Finally

In this article, I’ve written about a plan I came up with in the past.

It’s probably a pretty unusual story.

But what I want to get across isn’t just, “I used to think up something this messed up.”

Back then, I thought about suicide almost every day, repeatedly hurt myself, and felt intense hostility toward other people.

People who are mentally cornered don’t always show it to those around them.

I don’t want to worry anyone.
No one would understand.
There’s no point in talking to anyone.

Some people think that way and keep their problems to themselves.

And when you’re mentally cornered, extreme options you’d never normally consider can start to seem realistic.

I sometimes wonder whether things might have turned out differently if I’d been able to speak honestly with someone back then.

Of course, talking to someone doesn’t solve everything.

Even so, I think there’s value in talking to someone instead of carrying it all by yourself.

If someone close to you seems different from usual, try to keep an eye on them.

You don’t have to force them to tell you what’s wrong.

Just listening may mean a great deal to them.

At the very least, whenever I look back on who I was back then, I wish I’d talked to someone sooner.
